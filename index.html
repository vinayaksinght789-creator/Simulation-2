How it works

The common-emitter stage inverts the signal (180°). With K = Rc/R, the loaded ladder shifts the phase by another 180° at f = 1 / (2π·R·C·√(6 + 4K)). The transistor needs hFE ≥ 23 + 29/K + 4K, which bottoms out at about 44.5 when K ≈ 2.7. That is why a BJT oscillator needs far more gain than the 29 of an op-amp one: the collector resistor loads the ladder.

When hFE is above the minimum, the oscillation grows until the collector hits the supply or saturation, so the top and bottom of the wave flatten, and moving the bias point makes the clipping lopsided. Below the minimum it decays. Bias resistors are calculated automatically (divider current of ten times the base current, emitter at 10% of Vcc), and the ladder's loading of the base bias network is ignored.
